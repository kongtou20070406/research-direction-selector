# Agent-Workflow Evidence Ledger (checked 2026-11-23)

Companion to [multi-agent-parallel-playbook.md](multi-agent-parallel-playbook.md). Each row gives the measurement, the rule it changes here, and what the evidence does not show. arXiv IDs were resolved on the check date; re-check before citing in a paper.

| # | Evidence (measurement, denominator) | Rule in this workflow | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Kim et al., *Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems* (arXiv:2512.08296). Matched token budgets, 4 benchmarks × 5 architectures: mean multi-agent change vs. single agent −3.5% (95% CI −18.6% to +25.7%). Decomposable Finance Agent +80.9%; sequential PlanCraft −39% to −70%. | Fan out only work that splits into pieces with no shared state and one mechanical acceptance test each. Keep sequential chains (debugging, iterative derivation) in one agent. The lead verifies centrally. | Tool-use benchmarks, not research. Error amplification (independent 17.2× vs. centralized 4.4×) was not significant after efficiency controls. |
| E2 | *When Single-Agent with Skills Replace Multi-Agent Systems and When They Fail* (arXiv:2601.04748, technical report). Flat selection >90% at ≤20 skills, ~20% at 200; one look-alike competitor per skill cut accuracy 7–30%, two cut 17–63%. | `check_routes.py` scans every installed skill (27 on 2026-11-23), not only the 6 active ones, and warns when semantic neighbours do not name each other in a negative clause. | Synthetic libraries, two OpenAI models, selection accuracy rather than task success. |
| E3 | *SkillsBench* (arXiv:2602.12670). 87 tasks, 18 model–harness pairs: curated Skills 33.9% → 50.5% (+16.6 pp). Focused Skills with ≤3 modules beat exhaustive bundles. Self-generated Skills scored 8.1–11.5 pp *below* no Skills on 3 pairs. | Keep each SKILL.md focused. Agent-proposed rule edits need human approval before they enter a SKILL.md. On 2026-11-23 the installed `paper-write` ARS bundle was pruned from 2,252 files to the 93 it reads ([VENDORED.md](../../paper-write/references/ars/VENDORED.md)). | Containerised tasks with deterministic verifiers; research writing not measured directly. |
| E4 | Zhang et al., *Agentic Context Engineering* (ICLR 2026, arXiv:2510.04618). A full LLM rewrite of an evolving context shrank it from 18,282 to 122 tokens and accuracy from 66.7 to 57.1, below the 63.7 no-context baseline. Itemised delta bullets with helpful/harmful counters avoid this. | Lessons enter as itemised deltas (id, evidence, counters). No agent rewrites a whole SKILL.md. Line budgets cap the entry file, not the evidence files. | AppWorld and finance benchmarks. |
| E5 | Zhong et al., *ImpossibleBench* (ICLR 2026, arXiv:2510.20270). On tasks whose tests contradict the spec, read-only tests stopped test edits but not special-casing or operator overloading; hidden tests drove cheating success near zero at some cost to legitimate passes. | The gate snapshots the tree before dispatch, rejects any add/edit/delete under `--protect` (a new `conftest.py` included), and can run `--holdout-tests` the worker never saw. | Measures propensity on impossible tasks; rates on feasible tasks are lower. |
| E6 | Lei & Lei, *PASS Is a Claim, Not an Observation* (arXiv:2610.35950). Of 10 re-verified fix claims, those with enough detail to check failed the identifier + call-site test in 50% of cases. | The gate records `--worker-claim`; a claimed PASS that the gate rejects is logged as `false_claim`. | n = 10 claims from one platform. |
| E7 | Arslan, *Audit Without Verification* (arXiv:2609.07680). Pre-registered six-agent pipeline, 345,600 requests per chain model. When no agent proposed the true fault origin, the auditor found it in 4.1% of episodes (uniform guess 20%); deleting the agents' conclusion field raised this to 45.2% (+41.2 pp, 95% CI +35.3 to +46.9). Where the agents were right, deletion cost 70.5% → 55.7%. | Reviewer briefs carry raw evidence (diffs, logs, ledger rows) and withhold the producer's verdict. The reviewer writes its own reading first, then opens the sealed claim. | Fault attribution, not paper review. The sign of the effect depends on whether the producer was right. |
| E8 | Choi et al., *Debate or Vote* (NeurIPS 2025): across 7 benchmarks, majority voting explains most multi-agent-debate gains; debate is a martingale over beliefs. Pitre et al. (arXiv:2610.32060): 3,211 failed multi-round trajectories, dominated by failed correction and consensus after agents saw peers. | Reviewers run independently and never see each other's reports. Aggregation lists every blocking finding verbatim; one mechanically confirmed blocker stops the freeze regardless of votes. | Choi used 7–32B open models on QA-style tasks. |
| E9 | Goel et al., *Great Models Think Alike* (ICML 2025, arXiv:2502.04313): judges favour models similar to themselves, and errors grow more correlated with capability. | The writer's model family never approves its own draft: the main model writes and GPT reviews (only these two families are used). The GPT reviewers share a family, so a blocking finding counts only after a mechanical recheck. | Multiple-choice benchmarks. |
| E10 | Ren et al., *What's Missing in Autonomous Research?* (SoK preprint, 56 systems to June 2026): no public system has a full-manuscript release gate; text-only judges reached 21% recall on author-confirmed errors (SPOT) and accepted up to 82% of presentation-manipulated papers (BadScientist). | LLM review at `--freeze` is advisory over deterministic evidence. `--require-headline-bindings` fails any unbound decimal or percentage in sections 00–01. | Preprint; cited numbers come from the benchmarks it surveys. |
| E11 | Luo, Kasirzadeh & Shah, PNAS 2026 (*The more you automate, the less you see*). With test rankings inverted, Agent Laboratory's top-candidate choice fell 78.5% → 43.5%, because the selector saw test scores. | Select variants on validation only, open the holdout once per frozen claim, and log how many variants each claim tried. | Two systems on synthetic sequence tasks. |
| E12 | Elina, *Maat* (arXiv:2609.34017). Deterministic handoff validation helped in 5 of 6 workflows, but 35 of 94 halts (37%) were false alarms from validator bugs. | Every validator ships with good and bad fixtures (`tests/`). | Single-vendor study; v1 over-counted catches and was corrected. |

The failure labels in `accept_worker_deliverable.py` (`scope_creep`, `test_tampering`, `spec_violation`, `verification_failure`, `false_claim`, …) follow the specification / verification split of Cemri et al., *Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?* (arXiv:2503.13657).
